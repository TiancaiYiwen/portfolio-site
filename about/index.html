About me

I design learning experiences and study whether they work.

Frame 01

Background

I'm a Ph.D. student in Learning, Literacies and Technology at Arizona State University (expected May 2028). My research looks at how people learn with AI tools, especially how students handle uncertainty in science.

Before that, I earned an M.A. in Applied Linguistics at Teachers College, Columbia University, where I taught task-based English lessons to adult learners. I also hold a B.A. in English with a minor in Psychology from Wenzhou-Kean University.

Frame 02

How I approach design

[[Draft from her résumé. Yiwen should rewrite this in her own words.]]

  • Start with the learner. Before I design anything, I find out what learners already know and where they get stuck, through interviews, observation and surveys.
  • Practice over presentation. I build lessons around tasks and conversations instead of slides, whether that's a role-play for language learners or an AI tutor that asks questions instead of giving answers.
  • Measure what matters. I plan how to evaluate a design before building it, and I use the results to revise.

Frame 03

What I bring

  • Learner and needs analysis. Interviews, classroom observation, surveys and log analysis across several research projects.
  • Assessment and evaluation. Developed and validated a 23-item survey scale with more than 1,000 students; designed quizzes, pre/post surveys and rubric-aligned feedback.
  • Designing with AI. Co-designed two LLM-powered Socratic tutors used in a university biology course, including the prompting strategies behind their tutoring moves.
  • Teaching adult learners. Designed differentiated, task-based lessons for adult English learners and adjusted them based on learner feedback.
  • Working with stakeholders. Presented design tradeoffs to faculty and engineering collaborators, and wrote design recommendations for an engineering team.
